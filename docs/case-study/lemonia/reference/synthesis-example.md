# Research synthesis — reference answer

Sources: research/interviews.md (C1–C6), research/reviews.md (R1–R20).

## Themes

### 1. People cannot tell whether a pre-order was received (strong)
Four of six conversations and two comments.
- "I order on Instagram but I never know if you've seen the message." — C2
- "Ordered a cake on instagram, nobody answered for 2 days, called in the end." — R5
**Implication:** the pre-order flow must confirm on screen and by email, and
tell the person when they will hear back.

### 2. Visitors cannot read the offer (strong)
- "The pies had no labels in English. I pointed and hoped." — C3
- "English menu please!" — R14
**Implication:** a bilingual menu with prices is the most-needed page.

### 3. The current website is wrong, so it is not trusted (medium)
- "So I didn't trust the website." — C3
- "Open on Sunday afternoon?? Your website says no." — R7
**Implication:** hours on every page footer, updated from one place.

### 4. Workshops sell by word of mouth only (medium)
- "I would have booked if I'd seen it anywhere." — C4
- "Tried to buy a workshop as a gift, complicated." — R13
**Implication:** a workshops page with a shareable link per date, and a
gift option ("ask for a place for someone else").

### 5. Allergens are asked about every time (weak, one person + one comment)
- "I always have to ask if the koulourakia have almonds." — C2
- "Nut free options would be good to know in advance." — R8
**Implication:** allergens on the menu and a free-text allergy field on the
pre-order form, plus the kitchen's "may contain traces" note.

## Said by only one person
- Online payment would be welcome (C5). Owner has ruled it out for now.
- Text too small on websites (C6). Treat as an accessibility requirement anyway.
- Parking (R18). Out of scope; one line on the visit page.

## Least confident
Theme 5. Only one interview mentions allergies, but the cost of getting it
wrong is high, so it is in scope despite weak evidence.

## Answers to the owner's open questions
- **Greek or English first?** Greek first, with a visible language switch on
  every page; locals are the core and visitors look for the switch.
- **Allergies on the form?** Yes, as an optional free-text field.
- **Blog?** No evidence anyone wants one. Instead, a "What's in the oven"
  line on the home page that the owner can change weekly.
